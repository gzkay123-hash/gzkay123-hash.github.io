Singapore eSIM and number-retention checklist

For long stays, student travel, family visits, and business trips, it is usually safer to separate identity and data: keep the domestic number available for SMS/voice verification, then use a Singapore eSIM or local SIM for mobile data and hotspot needs.

  • Keep the domestic number active for banking, messaging, account recovery, and work verification.
  • Use the eSIM/local SIM as the default mobile-data line to avoid accidental roaming data charges.
  • Test SMS and backup verification methods before relying on them for important accounts.
  • Check whether the eSIM plan allows hotspot/tethering before a laptop-heavy trip.

300M vs 500M broadband decision notes

The difference between 300M and 500M broadband is most visible with concurrent devices, large downloads, NAS/cloud sync, high-bitrate 4K, and peak-hour headroom. If WiFi coverage, router ports, or 2.4GHz congestion are the real bottleneck, upgrading the plan alone may not change much.

  • Use wired or close-range WiFi 6 testing before judging the access plan.
  • Check upload speed if video calls, cloud backups, or remote work are the pain points.
  • Choose 500M when the price gap is small and multiple people often use high-bandwidth tasks.
  • Improve router placement, cabling, and 5GHz/WiFi 6 coverage before blaming the package.
